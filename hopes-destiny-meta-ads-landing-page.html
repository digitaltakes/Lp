What we treat

Mental health and substance use, treated together

Mental health conditions and substance use are deeply connected, and both deserve specialised care. When they appear together, which is common, we treat both in one integrated plan rather than sending you to two places.

ADHD, adults and teens Anxiety and panic Depression Trauma and PTSD Bipolar disorder Co-occurring disorders Alcohol use Prescription drug use Opioid and heroin use Cocaine and stimulant use Teen marijuana use Grief and life stressors

A co-occurring disorder means a mental health condition and a substance use disorder are present at the same time. Each one feeds the other, which is why we treat both together.

How we help

Three levels of outpatient care, and the ability to move between them

Your assessment determines where you start. As things improve you step down, and if you need more support you step back up, without changing provider or starting over with a new team.

Psychiatry

Psychiatric evaluation and medication management

Our physician and psychiatric nurse practitioner assess, diagnose, prescribe where appropriate, and adjust as your progress is monitored. Medication assisted treatment is available for alcohol and opioid use disorders.

Pharmacogenomics

GeneSight testing

Where it is clinically useful, GeneSight testing shows how your genetics may affect your response to particular psychiatric medications, which shortens the trial and error that many people dread most about starting treatment.

Assessment

Clinical and biopsychosocial assessment

Every plan starts with a full assessment covering history, current symptoms, family and social circumstances, so the level of care recommended is the one you actually need, not the one that is easiest to place you in.

Adjunctive therapies

Six therapies most outpatient centres nearby do not offer

These run alongside your clinical treatment, not instead of it. They engage the body as well as the mind, which matters for the many people who find talking about it is not enough on its own.

Yoga therapy

Gentle movement and breathwork to reconnect with the body, manage anxiety, depression and trauma responses, and build self awareness in a safe space.

Music therapy

Listening, songwriting and rhythm work that gives people a way to express feelings and process trauma without having to find the words first.

Art therapy

A safe way to explore emotions and process experiences through making things, particularly useful for people who struggle to say how they feel out loud.

Biofeedback

Real time monitoring that teaches control over physical responses such as heart rate and muscle tension. Effective for managing anxiety and trauma responses.

Pet therapy

Therapy animals reduce stress, anxiety and isolation, and create an easier way in for people managing depression or trauma.

Somatic re-experiencing

Gentle body awareness techniques that release physical trauma held in the body. Used for PTSD, complex trauma and long standing emotional difficulty.

Frequently asked questions

The answers people want before they pick up the phone

Do you accept Medicaid or Medicare? +

No. Hope's Destiny is not able to accept Medicaid or Medicare at this time. We work with private and commercial insurance plans, and we also accept private pay. If you are not sure which category your plan falls into, send us your details and we will check it for you at no cost.

Do you treat ADHD? +

Yes, for both adults and adolescents. Care can include psychiatric evaluation, diagnosis, medication management and ongoing therapy, and for students it can be scheduled around school hours. ADHD frequently appears alongside anxiety, depression or substance use, and where it does we treat those together rather than in isolation.

My teenager needs help. How does the adolescent program work? +

Teenagers aged 13 to 17 are treated in their own PHP and IOP groups, kept entirely separate from adult groups. Therapy is age appropriate, parents and caregivers are brought into the plan, and hours are arranged so that school continues wherever possible.

Do I need a referral to get started? +

No referral is needed. You can contact us directly to schedule an initial assessment. Our admissions team will guide you through the process and help determine the right level of care.

How do I know if I need professional help? +

It is worth reaching out if you are experiencing persistent changes in mood, behaviour or daily functioning that affect your quality of life. Common signs include:

  • Feeling overwhelmed, hopeless or unable to cope
  • Changes in sleep or eating patterns
  • Difficulty maintaining relationships or responsibilities
  • Using substances to manage emotions
  • Having thoughts of self harm

If you are in immediate crisis, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, available 24 hours a day.

What is a co-occurring disorder? +

A co-occurring disorder means someone is experiencing both a mental health condition and a substance use disorder at the same time. These conditions affect each other, so we treat both together to support long term recovery.

What kinds of therapy do you provide? +

Evidence based therapies including individual and group sessions, CBT, DBT and mindfulness techniques, alongside family support, couples therapy, medication management and psychiatric care. We also offer a full range of adjunctive therapies: yoga therapy, music therapy, art therapy, biofeedback, pet therapy and somatic re-experiencing.

Is treatment confidential? +

Absolutely. Your privacy is very important to us. All of your information is kept confidential in accordance with HIPAA and other legal standards.

Where are you, and how far do people travel? +

We are at 601 Dresher Road, Suite 105, Horsham, PA 19044, just off the highway with easy parking. Most of our clients come from Horsham and the surrounding Montgomery and Bucks County communities, including Abington, Doylestown, Lansdale, Warminster, Willow Grove and North Philadelphia.
