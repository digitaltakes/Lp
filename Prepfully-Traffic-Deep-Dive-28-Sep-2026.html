2. Why traffic has fallen

2.1 The whole interview-prep niche is losing US traffic

Five of the six interview-prep sites we compared lost between 43% and 68% of their estimated US organic traffic over the same year[1]. The exception is Hello Interview (+12%), which is built around system design, the one topic where US searches grew (+22% year on year)[6]. Prepfully's fall (-65%) is the second largest of the six.

2.2 Competitors gained top positions; Prepfully lost them

TryExponent, IGotAnOffer and Interviewing.io all rank for more keywords in Google's top 3 than a year ago, yet each lost 43% to 50% of its US traffic. That points to fewer clicks per search across the market. Prepfully and Interview Query lost top-3 keywords as well as traffic, and had the two largest falls[1][2].

For Prepfully, the fall sharpened after Google's core updates of 27 March and 21 May 2026[12]. US traffic went from 3,005 in March to 2,100 in May and 1,365 in September. Top-3 keywords fell from 123 in March to 86 in May, recovered to 120 in August, and fell again to 93 in September[1][2].

Guides took most of the ranking loss. Of the 134 keywords that dropped out, 97 belonged to interview guides, 24 to question-bank pages, 10 to the homepage and 3 to answer pages[4]. Examples include Databricks Software Engineer, Apple Product Manager, Uber Data Scientist, Netflix Engineering Manager and Google Technical Program Manager. The largest single loss was "interview coaching" (1,900 US searches a month), where the homepage dropped out from position 7.

Prepfully's Search Console data shows the same pattern: across the 88 Google, Meta and Amazon guides, clicks in the last 90 days were 3,128, against 4,659 at the 12-month pace[9].

2.4 What is not the cause

  • The copy sites. prepfully.live and prepfully.info have zero organic traffic in Ahrefs and were 2 to 3 weeks old on 22 September, so they cannot explain a decline that began a year earlier[10]. Google has indexed some of their pages and their login pages work, so they should still be blocked (action 4.5).
  • Site authority. Prepfully's Domain Rating rose from 30 to 35, and the number of websites linking to it from 685 to 1,392, over the same year[3]. Authority grew while traffic fell. (The linking-website total includes the spam network reviewed in August.)
  • Impression figures. On 8 to 10 September 2025 Google stopped showing 100 results per page to rank-tracking tools, which cut Search Console impressions for most sites[12]. Year-on-year comparisons should use clicks.
  • Off-topic traffic. An answers page ranks #1 for "gusto customer service" (7,800 US searches a month), from people looking for Gusto's support line[5]. Traffic like this does not produce bookings, so we leave it out of guide reporting.

3. What this means for link building

  1. Listing profiles have not helped rankings. Of the 120 listing rows in the tracker since August, 44 are marked nofollow and 3 dofollow; the rest are pending or already existed[11]. Over 90% of these links point to the homepage[12].
  2. The link gap on each guide is small. For the six target keywords, the competitor guides in Google's top 10 have between 0 and 26 linking websites each; Prepfully's guides have between 1 and 11[7][8]. Most need 2 to 6 more to match the competitor guide above them.
  3. First-hand posts hold the top spot. For 6 of the 8 guide keywords we checked, the #1 result is a personal interview account on Reddit, Medium or LinkedIn[7]. Prepfully's own Google TPM interview story on Medium, published in December 2025, ranks #3 today, above TryExponent's guide at #6.

How we will report it
Every Friday: each guide's Google position for its main search term, and a list of new links with the page linked, the link text and whether it is dofollow.

4.2 Publish the 10 links agreed with Erica

In July we agreed 10 links with Erica across five data-science websites (tesseract.academy and four partner sites). Six of the ten articles have passed the Pangram check and none is published yet[12]. Before each article goes out, we will choose the Prepfully guide that best matches its topic and write the link text around that guide's main search term. Where an article is about data science, the link will go to a data-science guide rather than being forced onto an engineering-manager guide, because a link on an unrelated topic does little for rankings.

What we will do
Finish the remaining four articles, match all ten to guides, and publish at most one per website per month.
What Prepfully gets
About two published links a month until all ten are live. These count towards 4.1.
How we will report it
For each of the ten: publication date, the guide it points to, and the link text.

4.3 Replace listing profiles with links to the six guides

Listing profiles mostly give nofollow links to the homepage[11], so they do not help the guides rank. From this week we stop creating them and use that time on four ways of getting a link to one of the six guides:

  1. The nine August replies. Write back to the nine websites that replied to our paid pitches in August[12] and offer each a free article on one of the six guide topics, with a link to that guide.
  2. Existing mentions. Find websites that already mention Prepfully without linking to it, and ask them to link to the matching guide.
  3. Topic-matched guest articles. Pitch free guest articles only to websites that accept the exact topic of one of the six guides.
  4. Two missing listings. Complete Capterra and G2, the two free listings that give dofollow links and that Prepfully does not have yet, then stop listing work.

Link swaps ("you link to us, we link to you") will stay rare, because Google's spam policy treats excessive link exchanges as link spam[12].

What Prepfully gets
Two to three free links a month to the six guides. With 4.2, this supplies the four to five links a month that 4.1 needs.
How we will report it
Every Friday: websites contacted, replies received and links published. Only links that point to one of the guides are counted.

4.4 Publish two first-hand interview stories a month on Medium

Google now places personal interview accounts from Reddit, Medium and LinkedIn at #1 for most guide searches, and Prepfully's own Google TPM story on Medium already ranks #3[7]. Each month we will publish two more, one per target role, based on a real candidate's or Prepfully coach's interview experience and shared with their permission. We will use the older Medium accounts, as agreed on 1 September[12]. Once a story is indexed and ranking, we add a link to the matching guide, as was done with the TPM story.

Medium links are nofollow, so the gain is an extra Prepfully-linked result on Google's first page and the visitors it sends, not ranking power for the guide. First-person writing is also the style that passes Pangram most reliably.

What we will do
Speak to the candidate or coach, write the story in their words, check it with Pangram, and publish it.
What we need from Prepfully
Two candidate or coach stories a month, through a short call or written notes.
How we will report it
Each story's Google position for its search term, and the visitors it sends to the guide.

4.5 Block the two copy sites

These are steps for Prepfully's developer:

  1. Block the copy sites at the server or CDN by refusing requests that arrive for prepfully.live or prepfully.info, or from the IP addresses they fetch from.
  2. Confirm that login and signup pages can no longer be opened through either domain.
  3. File a spam report with Google for both domains.

We will count how many pages of each domain Google still shows every week, until the count reaches zero[10].

4.6 Check expired domains before purchase

Three candidates have been checked so far. ledgergazette.com (344 dofollow linking websites) and 100interviews.com (212) look usable; prepare4interview.com is spam[12]. We will check any new candidate within two working days, including the exact pages that link to it. Google's spam policy defines expired domain abuse as follows[12]:

"an expired domain name is purchased and repurposed primarily to manipulate search rankings by hosting content that provides little to no value to users."

So, for any domain Prepfully buys:

  1. Never redirect it to prepfully.com.
  2. Build genuinely useful interview-prep content on it, on separate hosting.
  3. Add no links to Prepfully until the site has visitors of its own.
  4. Stay with the two domains now at auction until a review in March 2027.

6. Points for Prepfully's team

These fall outside link building but affect the same pages.

  • Low click-through on first-page guides. Meta Software Engineer gets 0.09% of searchers to click at position 8.4, Meta ML Engineer 0.08% at 12.3, Meta Data Engineer 0.03% at 13.2 and Google Software Engineer 0.46% at 7.8. The median for first-page guides with 1,000 or more impressions is 1.64%[9]. Their titles and descriptions are worth testing.
  • Off-topic pages. Consider whether the Gusto answers page should rank for a customer-support search at all[5].
  • "mock interview" (5,100 US searches a month) now ranks #10 through an articles page rather than the homepage[5]. Internal links could point that term back to the page meant to rank for it.
  • Refresh before links. Confirm each of the six target guides is up to date before its links go live.
